Punta Cana vs Cap Cana vs Santo Domingo: Where to Buy in Late 2026

The short answer:

  • Punta Cana is best for rental income at a lower entry price: about $2,000 to $3,500 per m2, with 8% to 12% net yields.
  • Cap Cana is best for appreciation and a premium lifestyle: about $3,000 to $4,500 per m2, with the strongest price growth of the three, up about 18% year over year.
  • Santo Domingo is best for steady, year-round demand: about $1,500 to $2,800 per m2 in the top neighborhoods, with 6% to 10% net yields and much less seasonality.

Every week, buyers ask me the same question: "Where should I actually buy?" As the fourth quarter of 2026 begins, the answer depends less on which area is "best" and more on what you want the property to do for you. That might be rental income, long-term growth, a home you use yourself, or a mix of the three.

Below is a side-by-side look at the Dominican Republic's three most popular markets, using our most recent neighborhood data and what we see every day working with international buyers.

Punta Cana: Best for Rental Income

Punta Cana has the deepest pool of vacation renters in the country and the widest choice of properties, from one-bedroom condos near the beach to large family villas. It is cheaper per square meter than Cap Cana, so the same budget buys more space or a better location.

  • Who it suits: buyers who want the property to pay for itself, and first-time Caribbean investors.
  • What to watch: supply. Many new buildings are being completed, so choose projects with a clear edge, such as beach access, a strong rental manager or a lower price per m2 than nearby buildings.
  • Timing: a purchase closed this fall can be furnished and listed before the December to April high season.

Cap Cana: Best for Growth and Lifestyle

Cap Cana is a gated, master-planned community with a marina, golf courses and beach clubs. It is the most expensive of the three markets, but it has also seen the fastest price growth, and its rental guests tend to pay higher nightly rates.

  • Who it suits: buyers who want a second home they enjoy using, with rental income on the side, and investors focused on resale value.
  • What to watch: HOA (building fee) costs are higher than average, so always compare net income, not gross. Check each project's delivery record before buying off-plan.
  • Entry point: one-bedroom apartments start in the mid-$200,000s, such as the GreenWood apartment at $260,000.

Santo Domingo: Best for Year-Round Demand

The capital works differently from the beach towns. Demand comes from business travel, companies moving staff and embassies, so rentals stay busy all year instead of peaking in winter. Neighborhoods such as Piantini, Naco and Evaristo Morales combine city living with solid rental demand.

  • Who it suits: buyers who want steadier monthly income, people relocating for work, and investors spreading risk beyond tourism.
  • What to watch: CONFOTUR applies only to selected projects in the city, so confirm approval for the specific building.
  • Price growth: Evaristo Morales led the city at about 16% year over year, from a lower starting price than Piantini.

How CONFOTUR Changes the Math

On an approved project, CONFOTUR (Law 158-01) waives the 3% transfer tax at purchase and the 1% annual property tax (IPI) on value above the exemption threshold for up to 15 years. On a $400,000 condo, that is about $12,000 saved at closing, plus about $2,300 a year in property tax. Because CONFOTUR is far more common in Punta Cana and Cap Cana, it often closes part of the yield gap with Santo Domingo. Read the full breakdown in our CONFOTUR tax savings guide.

Three Questions to Decide

  1. Will you use the property yourself? If you want beach time several weeks a year, choose Punta Cana or Cap Cana. If you will visit rarely, Santo Domingo's steady demand is worth a look.
  2. Income now or growth later? For income on a smaller budget, choose Punta Cana. For long-term value and a premium address, choose Cap Cana.
  3. How much seasonality can you accept? If uneven months would worry you, Santo Domingo smooths income across the year.

Many of our clients end up with a combination, such as a city apartment for steady income plus a resort condo they enjoy using in winter.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Punta Cana or Cap Cana better for a rental investment?

Punta Cana usually offers a lower entry price and slightly higher occupancy, which suits buyers focused on income. Cap Cana costs more per m2 but has shown the strongest price growth. Both typically produce 8% to 12% net yields on well-managed furnished rentals.

Is Santo Domingo a good place to buy property in 2026?

Yes, for buyers who want year-round demand. Central neighborhoods keep occupancy around 70% or more, with net yields of roughly 6% to 10%, and entry prices from around $198,000.

Does CONFOTUR apply in Santo Domingo?

Only for selected projects. It is much more common in tourism zones such as Punta Cana, Cap Cana, Bayahibe and La Romana.

Can foreigners buy property in the Dominican Republic?

Yes. Foreigners have the same ownership rights as Dominican citizens, with no quotas. Always have the title (Certificado de Título) reviewed and confirm property taxes are paid before closing.

When is the best time of year to buy?

For resort rentals, buying in the fall lets you list the property before the December to April high season. In Santo Domingo, timing matters less.
