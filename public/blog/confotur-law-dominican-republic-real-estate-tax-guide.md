## What is the CONFOTUR Law in the Dominican Republic?

The CONFOTUR Law (*Consejo de Fomento Turístico*) was enacted under Law 158-01 to stimulate foreign direct investment and tourism infrastructure across designated high-potential development zones in the Dominican Republic. 

While initially designed to incentivize major hospitality operators and master-planned resort communities, the law extends substantial, direct tax incentives to individual purchasers of residential units within approved developments.

For international buyers acquiring real estate in the Dominican Republic, buying a CONFOTUR-certified property delivers a level of fiscal optimization that few other Caribbean destinations can match.

## Key Tax Exemptions for Property Buyers Under CONFOTUR

When acquiring a luxury residence or investment property certified under Law 158-01, buyers benefit from two major tax exemptions that create immediate and long-term financial advantages:

### 1. 0% Property Transfer Tax (Immediate 3% Savings)

In standard real estate transactions in the Dominican Republic, buyers are subject to a one-time property transfer tax (*Impuesto de Transferencia Inmobiliaria*) equal to 3% of the government-assessed value of the property or purchase price.

Under CONFOTUR certification:
- **The 3% transfer tax is 100% exempt.**
- When purchasing a $500,000 luxury residence, this represents an immediate cash savings of $15,000 upon title transfer.
- On a $1,500,000 beachfront estate, your direct upfront tax savings total $45,000.

### 2. 15-Year Exemption from Annual Property Tax (IPI)

Residential property owners in the Dominican Republic typically pay an annual Real Estate Property Tax (*Impuesto al Patrimonio Inmobiliario* or IPI) of 1% on the cumulative value of real estate assets exceeding the government-set threshold (approximately DOP $9.8M, or ~$165,000 USD, adjusted annually for inflation).

Under CONFOTUR certification:
- **Owners receive a complete waiver from the 1% annual IPI tax for up to 15 years.**
- For luxury residences and premium rental assets, this exemption preserves thousands of dollars in operating income each year, substantially boosting net rental yields (*ROI*).
- Over a standard 15-year holding horizon, the cumulative savings from IPI exemptions often equal 15% or more of the initial property value.

## Additional Incentives for Corporate and Commercial Investors

For institutional investors, developers, and corporate entities acquiring land or commercial hospitality assets, CONFOTUR also provides:

- **Exemption from corporate income tax** on qualifying tourism and hospitality revenues for up to 15 years.
- **Exemption from import duties and national taxes** on construction materials, furnishings, and equipment necessary for property development.
- **Exemption from capital increase and incorporation taxes** when establishing property holding entities in the Dominican Republic.

## How to Verify if a Property Qualifies for CONFOTUR

Not all properties in the Dominican Republic qualify for CONFOTUR benefits. To ensure your intended acquisition is fully certified and compliant, consider the following verification steps:

### 1. Request the Official CONFOTUR Resolution
Every approved development receives a formal ministerial resolution issued by the Ministry of Tourism (*MITUR*) and the Ministry of Finance (*Hacienda*). Request a copy of this resolution alongside the development's official certification number.

### 2. Review the Exemption Expiration Date
CONFOTUR benefits run for a fixed period of up to 15 years from the initial date of project approval. It is critical to confirm how many years remain on the development's active tax exemption cycle.

### 3. Conduct Legal Due Diligence with Specialized Counsel
Prior to executing a purchase agreement or wiring funds, engage independent legal counsel and an experienced real estate advisory firm. Professional due diligence ensures that the developer has completed all registry filings and that tax exemptions will transfer smoothly onto your individual property title.
