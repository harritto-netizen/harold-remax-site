Chapter 01

The four markets that matter

The Dominican Republic is not one market. Where you buy shapes the return profile more than what you buy. Here is how the four premium regions compare in 2026.

Cap Cana

Gated master-planned resort next to Punta Cana International Airport. Best-in-class golf, marina, and beach clubs. Premium for turn-key ocean-view villas and branded residences. Strongest short-term rental yields on the island (7–10% net after HOA).

Punta Cana (Bavaro, Cocotal, Los Corales)

The volume market. More inventory, wider price range, higher rental turnover. Ideal for first-time investors and buyers who want liquidity when they exit. Newer developments frequently qualify for CONFOTUR.

Santo Domingo (Piantini, Naco, Evaristo Morales)

Domestic-demand market: urban condos and penthouses for locals, expats, and diplomats. Long-term tenants, dollar-denominated rents in the premium buildings, low seasonality. Lower yields (4–6%) but the most stable.

Casa de Campo & La Romana

Legacy luxury: private homes on golf holes, marina front, ultra-low-density. Trades on prestige more than yield. Sellers are patient, so pricing rarely corrects.

Field note Cap Cana and Punta Cana price per square meter are within 12% of each other on paper, but rental income differs by 40%+. Ask for actual 12-month rental history before you offer — not projections.

Chapter 02

CONFOTUR & the tax picture

Law 158-01 (CONFOTUR) is the single most powerful lever in Dominican real estate. On qualifying properties it eliminates three taxes at once.

TaxStandard rateWith CONFOTUR
Transfer tax (one-time, at closing)3% of price0%
Annual property tax (IPI, over ~US$175k)1% per year0% for 15 years
Income tax on rental income27%0% for 15 years

On a US$500,000 property held for 15 years and rented 60% of the year, the difference is roughly US$180,000–$220,000 in taxes saved. It is not marginal — it changes which deals make sense.

What qualifies

  • Developments explicitly registered under CONFOTUR — ask for the resolution number and dates.
  • New construction or first resale. Second-hand transfers usually lose the benefit.
  • Registered in your personal name or a properly structured Dominican company.
Watch out Many "CONFOTUR" claims from brokers are outdated. The registration expires. Ask the seller's attorney for the current Consejo de Fomento Turistico certificate dated within the last 12 months.

Chapter 03

The 7-step buying process

1 · Discovery & shortlist (1–2 weeks)

Define budget, use case (personal, rental, hybrid), and non-negotiables. Tour 4–8 properties across two regions before committing to one.

2 · Reservation agreement (1 day)

A reservation deposit (typically 1% or US$5,000–10,000) takes the property off market for 10–15 days. Fully refundable if title or legal review turns up defects.

3 · Due diligence (10–20 days)

Your attorney (not the seller's) verifies title at the Registry of Titles, checks for liens, encumbrances, HOA arrears, and confirms CONFOTUR status. Never skip this.

4 · Promise of sale (Promesa de Venta)

The binding purchase contract. Typically 10–20% down. Specifies closing date, penalties, and conditions precedent (financing, permits, etc.).

5 · Balance & deed (Contrato de Venta)

Balance paid, notarized deed signed. For foreign buyers a Power of Attorney lets your attorney close on your behalf — you do not need to fly back.

6 · Registry & title transfer

Deed filed at the Registry of Titles. New title (Certificado de Titulo) issued in your name in 30–90 days.

7 · Handover & keys

Utility transfers, HOA registration, and, if renting, onboarding with a property manager. Budget 2–4 weeks after closing before it is truly turn-key.

Chapter 05

Financing as a foreigner

Foreign buyers can and do get Dominican mortgages — but the terms and paperwork differ from the US, Canada, or Europe.

  • Down payment: Expect 30–40% minimum for non-residents. Residents with local income can go lower.
  • Rates (2026): USD mortgages 7.5–9.5%. DOP mortgages 11–14%. Fixed for 3–5 years, then variable.
  • Term: Typically 15–20 years. Some banks cap at age 70 at maturity.
  • Documents: Passport, tax returns (last 2 years), bank statements (6 months), employment or income verification, and a Dominican tax ID (RNC or Cedula). Plan on 45–75 days to close.
Alternative Developer financing on pre-construction is often more competitive than bank financing: 20–30% down, progress payments over 24–36 months, zero interest until delivery. On the right project it is the cheapest capital available.

Chapter 06

Ten mistakes to avoid

  • Using the seller's lawyer. Always retain your own independent attorney. Non-negotiable.
  • Trusting verbal CONFOTUR claims. Get the certificate on file, dated.
  • Skipping the title search. Certificado de Titulo alone is not enough; check the Registry directly for liens filed after issuance.
  • Signing in English only. The Spanish text is what governs. Have both.
  • Wiring funds internationally without a receiving Dominican bank account first. Delays closing 2–4 weeks and triggers compliance reviews.
  • Under-budgeting HOA increases. Ask for 3 years of HOA financials and pending assessments.
  • Buying in a project with under 40% sold. HOA math breaks and completion risk climbs.
  • Ignoring rental restrictions. Some communities cap or ban short-term rentals; verify in the HOA reglamento, not the sales pitch.
  • Assuming projected rental income. Ask for last 12 months of actual bookings from three comparable units.
  • Closing without title insurance. Optional in the DR, but for US$1,500–3,000 it removes a real category of risk.

Chapter 07

Pre-offer checklist

Before you sign a reservation on any property, confirm every item below.

  • Title (Certificado de Titulo) is in the seller's name and matches the ID.
  • No liens, mortgages, or embargoes at the Registry of Titles.
  • Property taxes (IPI) paid current or CONFOTUR-exempt with dated certificate.
  • HOA fees paid current; 3 years of financials reviewed.
  • Building permits and habitability certificate on file (for new construction).
  • Utility accounts (electric, water, telecom) with no outstanding debt.
  • Zoning & short-term rental status verified in writing.
  • Comparable sales pulled for the last 12 months in the same building or block.
  • Actual 12-month rental history from three comparable units.
  • Independent attorney retained — not the seller's.
